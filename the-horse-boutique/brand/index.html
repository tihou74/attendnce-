Visual identity system, version 0.1. Logo suite, palette, typography and application rules.

01 — The idea

A tack room, not a tack shop.

The identity is built on the three materials every rider already trusts: oxblood leather, polished brass, and the ivory of clean breeches. Nothing is invented; everything is quoted from the sport.

The mark is a stirrup iron holding the letter H. A stirrup is the one object shared by every discipline and every price point in the catalogue — dressage, jumping, endurance, or a first pony. It is the point where rider and equipment meet, which is precisely what a boutique does.

The house voice is unhurried. Wide letter-spacing, hairline rules, generous margins, and a serif that has been used on saddlery brass plates for two centuries. Restraint is the luxury signal — not gradients, not gloss, not ornament.

03 — Clear space & minimum size

Give it room.

Minimum clear space on every side equals half the height of the mark (token: --logo-clearspace-ratio: 0.5). Nothing enters that zone — not type, not imagery, not a page edge.

04 — Correct use

Five ways to ruin it.

The examples below are deliberately wrong. Each one is the mistake most likely to reach print.

Do

Ivory on oxblood. Full contrast, original proportions, no effects.

Don't

Never stretch or condense. Scale proportionally only.

Don't

Never rotate or tilt. The stirrup hangs level.

Don't

Never recolour outside the palette. Oxblood, ivory, brass, ink.

Don't

No shadows, bevels, glows or gradients on the mark.

Don't

Never place tone-on-tone. The silhouette must stay legible.

07 — The two registers

One house, two voices.

A boutique that carries both hand-stitched leather and carbon-shell helmets needs to speak in two tempos without splitting in half. Both registers share the same palette and the same marks. Only the typographic lead, the tracking, the density and the neutral temperature change — never the hue. Apply with data-register="performance".

08 — Applications

How it lives.

The mark is monochrome by rule: one ink, one ground. That is what makes it survive foil, deboss, embroidery and laser engraving without a single redraw.

09 — Open items

Awaiting the brand roster.

Blocked on input

The final calibration of this system depends on the roster of brands the boutique carries under contract. That list has not been received yet.

If the roster is Hermès / Cavalleria Toscana / Sergio Grasso tier — the heritage register becomes the only register, brass moves to real foil, and the serif takes every headline. If it includes Kask / Samshield performance labels — the performance register is promoted to equal status and gets its own category architecture. If the mix is broad — the dual-register system above ships as designed.

A “Brands We Carry” section, a partner-logo lockup rule and a co-branding clear-space spec will be added to this document once the roster arrives.

Deliberately not shipped

Horse-head silhouette. A head mark was drafted and rejected. A convincing equine profile depends on the jowl mass and a tight crop; two drafts read closer to a giraffe than a horse, and a weak figurative mark would cost more credibility than the extra asset is worth. The stirrup, bit and horseshoe already cover every use a head mark would have served. If a head mark is still wanted it should be drawn as a dedicated illustration and reviewed on its own, not bundled into this release.
